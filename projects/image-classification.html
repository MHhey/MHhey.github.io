Efficient image classification for field hardware

Fine-tuned a compact MobileNetV2 classifier with staged transfer learning, targeting the accuracy, latency, and power limits of cameras and handheld devices used in the field.

RoleSole author
StackPython · TensorFlow · Keras
DataCIFAR-100

Overview

Vision on a construction site runs on the hardware already there, such as fixed cameras, handheld devices, and drones, under power and latency budgets no large backbone will meet. MobileNetV2's depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks target that regime. This project measures what accuracy costs at that parameter count.

Environment

  • Framework: TensorFlow / Keras
  • Backbone: MobileNetV2, ImageNet pretrained weights
  • Dataset: CIFAR-100: 100 classes, 500 training images per class, 32×32 RGB

Implementation

  1. Input resolution. CIFAR-100 is 32×32; MobileNetV2 expects considerably larger input. Upsampling strategy is decided first and materially affects the result.
  2. Frozen-backbone baseline. Train the classification head only, establishing what the pretrained features are worth on this task before modifying them.
  3. Staged fine-tuning. Progressively unfreeze upper blocks at a reduced learning rate. Unfreezing everything at once destroys the pretrained representation.
  4. Augmentation. Geometric and photometric augmentation to compensate for 500 images per class.

Notes

  • Top-1 accuracy alone is the wrong target for this regime. The operative metric is accuracy per unit latency, power or inference cost, and which one you pick changes the model.
  • Failures cluster on visually confusable classes rather than distributing evenly across the 100. That structure indicates where to spend effort.
  • Transfers directly to construction site imagery: many similar classes, limited labelled data, non-GPU inference targets.
