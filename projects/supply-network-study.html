Supplier selection under blockchain transparency

Built a research platform that puts construction professionals through real supplier selection decisions under different kinds of supply chain information, to measure what actually changes who they pick and whether they go direct or through an intermediary.

RoleLead author, sole designer and builder of the platform
StackHTML · CSS · JavaScript
MethodControlled behavioral experiment, IRB 25-1022
CodeGitHub repository

Overview

Surveys are the usual way to ask whether more supply chain transparency changes buying behavior. This platform instead puts a participant through a real weekly decision. Each week they choose a supplier from a live network, under a specific information condition, and the platform records what they actually picked.

How it works

A participant moves through a sequence of weekly scenarios, tracked with a progress bar at the top of the screen. In each week they see a supply network as an interactive diagram, a central node for themselves, connected to supplier and logistics nodes. Clicking a node opens a panel with that supplier's information, and the amount of information available changes with the week's transparency condition, from a baseline with only standard supplier information, up to conditions with fuller visibility into supplier performance and standing.

Four conditions were tested. A baseline showing only standard attributes such as delivery reliability, price range, and capacity. Transparency, adding real-time operational visibility. Reputation, adding collective judgement from past performance. Verification, adding credentials and formal certification. Beyond the choice itself, the platform logs decision time, confidence on a seven point scale, and every node inspection, so the record covers how someone arrived at a decision rather than only what they landed on.

What it found

  • Trust is a property of what you can see, not of the supplier. The same supplier, with the same attributes, got treated as a different firm depending on what buyers could observe about them. Supplier qualification is therefore partly a question of designing visibility, not just vetting the vendor.
  • Intermediaries fill a visibility gap, not a logistics one. Reliance on middlemen collapsed once buyers could see supplier operations directly. If you want to buy direct, the lever is making performance legible rather than negotiating the distributor out.
  • Credentials and track record are sequential, not competing. Certification did real work while a relationship had no history, then stopped mattering once behavior was visible. Prequalification that never gives way to performance data keeps paying for a signal that has already gone quiet.

Notes

  • This is a study for my PhD dissertation, and sits alongside the agentic bullwhip simulation as a second angle on the same question, how information availability changes behavior in a construction supply chain, this time with human decision makers instead of simulated agents.
  • Built and run solo, including the network visualisation, scenario logic, and consent handling.
