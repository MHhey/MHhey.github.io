Supplier selection under blockchain transparency

Built a research platform that puts people through real supplier selection decisions under different levels of blockchain-enabled supply chain transparency, to measure how visibility actually changes what a buyer chooses, not just what they say they would choose.

RoleSole designer and builder
StackHTML · CSS · JavaScript
StudyDissertation, study 3
CodeGitHub repository

Overview

Surveys are the usual way to ask whether more supply chain transparency changes buying behaviour, and people are unreliable narrators of their own decisions under hypothetical conditions. This platform instead puts a participant through a real weekly decision. Each week they choose a supplier from a live network, under a specific information condition, and the platform records what they actually picked rather than what they say they would pick.

How it works

A participant moves through a sequence of weekly scenarios, tracked with a progress bar at the top of the screen. In each week they see a supply network as an interactive diagram, a central node for themselves, connected to supplier and logistics nodes. Clicking a node opens a panel with that supplier's information, and the amount of information available changes with the week's transparency condition, from a baseline with only standard supplier information, up to conditions with fuller visibility into supplier performance and standing.

  • Network view an SVG diagram of the buyer, suppliers, and third-party logistics nodes, with the active selection highlighted.
  • Scenario progression a multi-week sequence, each week gated until the previous one is complete, so conditions are shown in a controlled order.
  • Information panel supplier detail that expands with the week's transparency condition, isolating what changes participant behaviour.
  • Consent flow a separate consent screen ahead of the study proper, since this collects human-subject data.

Why it is built this way

The dependent variable is a choice, not an opinion, so the platform had to be a real interface a participant clicks through rather than a questionnaire describing one. Building it as a standalone web app also meant the information shown at each node could be controlled precisely by condition, which is what makes the comparison across weeks meaningful.

Notes

  • This is study 3 of my dissertation, and sits alongside the agentic bullwhip simulation as a second angle on the same question, how information availability changes behaviour in a construction supply chain, this time with human decision makers instead of simulated agents.
  • Built and run solo, including the network visualisation, scenario logic, and consent handling.
